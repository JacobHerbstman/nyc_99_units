\section{Empirical Strategy}

The analysis begins with exact parent-size distributions. I compare proposals filed from 2011 through 2022 with proposals filed after 485-x took effect. Raw counts show total development volume. Annualized counts put periods of different length on a common time scale. Normalized densities divide by total project mass, isolating changes in the shape of the size distribution from citywide changes in construction activity.

Changes in the kinds of sites developed can also shift the distribution. I therefore reweight the historical sample to match the post-policy distribution of predetermined lot, zoning, and location characteristics. Realized unit count never enters the weights because it is the developer's choice margin. The resulting comparison is a composition-adjusted descriptive benchmark, not yet a causal estimate of projects' no-policy unit counts.

The second part of the analysis examines splitting directly. It measures whether filings that share zoning lots, adjacent parcels, dates, owners, applicants, or architects form parent developments near 99, 150, or 198 units. This parent-level construction prevents two 99-unit filings from being mistaken for two independent projects when the available evidence indicates one 198-unit development.
