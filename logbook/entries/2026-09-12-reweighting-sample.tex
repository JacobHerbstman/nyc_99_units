\section*{September 12, 2026: Which parents enter the reweighting sample?}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry.}

The current reweighting sample excludes nine rental parents solely because
multiple additive filings share a building identification number (BIN): two
historical parents and seven post-period parents. These parents have complete
lot coverage. The calibration summary labels all exclusions as incomplete
features, so that label does not describe the full selection rule.

Among the adopted rental parents with at least 50 units, the canonical panel
contains 562 historical parents from 2019--2022 and 333 post-period parents from
January 2025--July 8, 2026. The composition-eligibility rule leaves 560 and 309,
respectively. The historical exclusions are the two repeated-BIN cases. The
post-period exclusions comprise seven repeated-BIN cases and 17 parents with
incomplete lot coverage. Lot coverage requires a matched parcel with positive
area for every source filing in the parent; this includes source filings whose
units are not additive.

The repeated-BIN exclusions include three post-period parents recorded as
$99+99$: 229 Shepherd Avenue/240 Highland Place, 2433 Atlantic Avenue/28 Havens
Place, and 937/951 East 108 Street. They also include the $99+99+99+99$ parent at
54/62 Sullivan Place and 73/99 Empire Boulevard. This rule therefore affects
the organizational outcomes central to the joint model. A shared BIN warrants
checking what the identifier represents and whether the retained filings
duplicate a building. Its role in sample selection remains a research question.

Recomputing the calibration from the current canonical panel reproduces the
saved weights to floating-point precision (maximum absolute difference
$2.22\times10^{-16}$). The weights sum to 309. This verifies the saved
calculation for the selected sample; adopting it for estimation also requires
resolving the sample restrictions and the timing of the matched site features.

reweighting. The site-characteristics producer now joins DOB initial filings
by root job number and uses each observed DOB BIN, with HDB fallback when it is

\subsection*{Follow-up: treating lot assembly as a potential policy response}

Jacob raised the concern that multi-lot status is likely affected by the
policy and should not be assumed away. The baseline now calibrates historical
parents on log lot area, residential FAR, built FAR and borough. Lot counts
and constituent configurations remain in the data as observed characteristics
of development choices. The eligible sample remains 561 historical and 316
post-period parents; Bond Street remains ineligible.

Matching multi-lot status had given historical parents the post-period
multi-lot share of 9.49 percent. The revised weights yield 2.28 percent.
Although multi-lot status and multiple constituent buildings are different
variables, their relationship materially affects the comparison: the weighted
historical share with multiple constituents falls from 10.53 to 3.46 percent,
against 16.14 percent observed post-policy. The historical exact-99 share
falls from 1.94 to 1.34 percent, against 12.34 percent observed. These are
changes in the constructed comparison, not identified policy effects.

The remaining site measures also depend on parent boundaries. Inspection of
the producer confirms that land area sums distinct matched lots, while
residential and built FAR are weighted averages over those same lots using
their land areas. Historical parcel attributes come from filing-specific
lagged vintages; post-period attributes come from 23v3.1. The underlying
parcel attributes therefore predate the filing, but which lots enter the
parent aggregate can reflect a later assembly choice. This remaining
assumption needs review before treating the weighted comparison as a settled
causal benchmark. The framework and figure descriptions now name the archival
parcel measures and explain the boundary issue.

The revised calibration has positive weights ranging from 0.138 to 1.672,
effective sample size 495.74, and maximum absolute matched-total error
$9.45\times10^{-10}$. Weights sum to 316 within numerical tolerance. The
499-replication bootstrap has 489 successful calibrations and 10 recorded
failures. The parent and constituent panels are unchanged. Reweighted
distributions, bootstrap intervals, meeting figures, framework values, and
the dependent placebo and historical-window diagnostics were rebuilt. No
structural estimation has begun.

Jacob specified that the parent site's land footprint is taken as given:
the developer chooses units and organization within that footprint. The
