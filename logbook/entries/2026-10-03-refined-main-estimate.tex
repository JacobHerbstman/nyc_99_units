\section*{October 3, 2026: The main model refined off the grid}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry.}

On the grid the main model's units lost moved between 760 and 852 as $\gamma$
changed, although the fit hardly did: units lost are sensitive along the flat
top of the likelihood, and the grid steps in $\kappa$ (0.02) and $s$ (0.5)
landed the estimate on a low point. With $\gamma$ fixed the likelihood is
smooth in $\kappa$, $s$, $\sigma$ and $\varepsilon$, so the main model is now
estimated in two steps: the grid search, then at each $\gamma$ on its grid a
continuous search from the best grid point, and the best $\gamma$ kept
(\path{refine_scaled_burden} in \path{notch_model.R}). The search uses L-BFGS-B
within the range of the grid. In seven placebo draws with no burden an
unbounded search drifted ($\sigma$ to 10,000) without converging, and a
search clamped to the box stalled on its flat edges. The grid point remains
the estimate when it fits better, as in the placebo, whose best fit is no
burden. Every bootstrap draw is estimated the same way. The alternatives stay
at their grid estimates and are compared with the main model's grid estimate,
kept as \texttt{scaled\_burden\_grid}.

The parameters barely move and the fit improves by 0.05 log points. Units
lost rise by 6 percent, to near the bootstrap median (about 830 in both), and no
longer depend on $\gamma$. Draw by draw the refined and grid estimates of units
lost correlate at 0.99 and the spread across draws is unchanged, so the width
of the interval is sampling noise, not the grid. Units lost per 100 recent
parents on the checks are 290 (main), 226 (180-day horizon), 240 (2025
parents), 0 (placebo) and 282 (2014--2022 history), against 274, 222, 233, 0
and 271 on the grid.

\paragraph{Comparison with Garicano, Lelarge and Van Reenen (2016).} Their
replication code (\path{plfit_baseline}, openICPSR 112909) fits a closed-form
likelihood by Newton's method from typed-in starting values, on transformed
parameters like ours, with the returns to scale $\theta$ calibrated because
the likelihood is flat in it. $\gamma$ plays that role here: it is not
identified, and units lost are reported across it. Their standard errors are
clustered sandwich errors; the bootstrap is kept here because the weights carry
sampling noise from the historical parents that the curvature of the
likelihood would ignore.

\paragraph{Cleanup.} The least-squares fit no longer writes moments, cell fit,
profiles or near-optimal ranges, which nothing read; it keeps its estimates,
units lost with building counts held fixed, and the direct gap in units.
