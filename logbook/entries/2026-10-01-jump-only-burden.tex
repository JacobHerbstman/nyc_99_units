\section*{October 1, 2026: The burden is a jump only}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry.}

Jacob dropped the kink from the model, so the burden is a jump $\kappa$ on each
building of 100 units or more, scaled by developer. The main model had
estimated the kink at zero (bootstrap interval 0--0.10), and the jump alone
keeps the notation and the code simple. With $J$ buildings the burden is zero
up to $99J$ units and $\kappa$ above, since one building takes the rest. Without
a kink, letting the jump vary by developer is the main model, so that
alternative is gone.

The main model and its splitting-cost variants are unchanged: jump 0.18,
spread 2, growth 2, mean splitting cost 0.20, 761 units lost, with one fewer
parameter. Only the models with one burden for everyone used the kink, as their
one way to make the buildings that stay above 100 smaller. The single jump now
loses 496 units against 1,130, and fits 3 log points worse; least squares loses
636 against 1,018. Against the single jump, a jump that varies by developer
improves the log-likelihood by 10 points, against 7 before.
