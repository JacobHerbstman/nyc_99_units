# Audit the 150-unit 485-x threshold

This audit asks why the annual number of proposed parent developments at or
above 150 units is higher in the 2025--2026 filing cohort than in the
2017--2019 pre-pandemic Affordable New York cohort.

The audit distinguishes all linked 6-plus-unit parents from the classified
A/B rental-opportunity sample. It reports exact one-unit distributions,
borough composition, 485-x exposure status, single- versus multiple-filing
parents, and a case ledger for every post-policy 150-plus parent.

The case ledger also distinguishes the linked economic parent from HPD's
registered eligible site. A linked parent can exceed 150 units while separate
component buildings are registered under Option B. Those cases are retained
and flagged rather than being relabeled as Option A from the parent total.

Because historical and post-policy parent linkage can differ, the audit also
reports the distribution at the original component-filing level. Component
filings are not assumed to equal eligible sites, but comparing both levels
reveals whether an apparent parent-size change is generated by aggregation.

The yearly linkage diagnostic uses historical parents through 2022 and the
same DOB-based parent construction for 2023–2026. DOB filings begin in 2022 as
padding, so the 2023 and 2024 comparison cohorts have complete symmetric
365-day linkage windows.

For post-policy DOB NOW filings, the audit also assigns the statutory Zone A
or Zone B wage geography from the filing's 2020 NTA. Historical HDB filings do
not carry the same NTA field, so the zone breakdown is descriptive for the
post-period case ledger rather than a historical zone-specific estimate.

The outputs are diagnostics. A high post-policy count above 150 does not by
itself identify a causal response to 485-x; it can reflect a broad development
cycle, project composition, zoning geography, or policy responses at both 99
and 150 units.
