\section{Introduction}

New York City's 485-x housing tax incentive changes the cost of building at 100 residential units. Projects below the threshold face one set of affordability and labor requirements, while projects at or above it face a different regime. Developers can respond by changing a project's size, combining buildings into a larger development, or dividing one development opportunity across multiple filings.

This paper studies those project-design responses. The first evidence is descriptive: I compare the distribution of proposed project sizes before and after 485-x, reconstructing the economic parent when related filings appear to form one development. The analysis asks whether the post-policy distribution places unusual mass at 99 units and at multiples of 99, and whether apparent bunching survives after accounting for changes in the scale and parcel composition of development.
