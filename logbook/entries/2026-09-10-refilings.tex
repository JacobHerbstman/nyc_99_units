\section*{September 10, 2026: Count refiled buildings once and retain their original dates}
\textit{Agent-drafted implementation of Jacob's requested automatic detection and original-date convention.}

Automatic detection identifies 12 withdrawn applications with subsequent replacement filings in 11 parents. Counting only the replacements removes 464 duplicate units across the retained filing universe. Six of these pairs account for the previously flagged 368 units across five estimation parents. Parent identities and original cohort dates are unchanged. Four previously reviewed multi-building parents now correctly contain one additive building, leaving 67 multi-constituent parents in the current complete-characteristic A/B review.

A match requires the same valid building identifier, recorded owner and applicant, and a unique non-withdrawn replacement filed after the recorded withdrawal. The code stops on ambiguous matches or a match outside an existing parent. It does not infer replacements from nearby addresses or similar unit counts, and it does not discard withdrawn proposals without a qualifying replacement. Existing manually reviewed alternative designs remain a separate category.

The constructor retains both source records and their raw units and dates. Only the replacement contributes units. In the constituent panel, the filing date is the original proposal date; a separate record date retains the replacement application's actual date. The refiling indicator is true for a detected refiling, with its date recorded separately and missing otherwise. At parent level, the cohort date remains the original date and the refiling date is the earliest qualifying refiling among its constituents. For 2279 University Avenue, this keeps October 1, 2025 as the original date and April 8, 2026 as the refiling date, with 48 units counted once rather than 96.

These indicators describe qualifying refilings observed in the saved July 2026 DOB records and retained filing universe. A false indicator does not rule out a refiling outside that coverage. Retaining an original proposal date while using replacement units is the adopted observation convention; it does not reconstruct the original design's unit choice. Historical source coverage and the other outstanding parent-boundary and unit-vintage questions remain distinct. Estimation remains on hold.
