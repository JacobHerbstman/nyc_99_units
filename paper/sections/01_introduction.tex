\section{Introduction}

This paper studies the incidence of New York City's 485-x housing tax incentive. The program creates a sharp threshold at 100 residential units. Projects below the threshold face one set of affordability requirements, while projects at or above the threshold face more stringent affordability and construction wage requirements.

The empirical question is whether the threshold is capitalized into land prices or instead appears as costly developer avoidance, including bunching at 99 units, delay, larger units, or adjacent split filings.
