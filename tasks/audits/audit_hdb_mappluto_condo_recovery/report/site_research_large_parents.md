## 54/62 Sullivan Place and 73/99 Empire Boulevard

The four saved jobs are B01316595, B01316678, B01317227, and B01339675. The
first three were filed December 4, 2025; the fourth was filed December 30. Each
records 99 units. Their filing lots are Brooklyn block 1306, lots 25, 30, 20,

The selected 23v3.1 parcel map predates the filings and contains only lot 28 at
this location. Its recorded area is **38,118 square feet** and its measured
polygon is about 39,218 square feet. The latter is a cartographic measurement,
not the administrative lot-area value. DOF's dated history shows that lot 28
was formed in 2021 by merging former lots 28, 32, and 35. The change was entered
July 19, 2021 under transaction 93082 and cites the deed recorded April 27,
2021, CRFN 2021000151468. This establishes that the entire candidate site was
one tax lot years before the four filings.

The March 2, 2026 zoning-lot ownership statement, recorded March 9 as document
2026030900544002, resolves the mapping. The supervising researcher independently
read the actual ACRIS scan. Page 3 unequivocally subdivides old lot 28 into
tentative lots 20, 25, 28, and 30, labels all four filing addresses, and draws
the four lots as quadrants within one complete trapezoid. Its outer dimensions
are 142.98 feet along Sullivan Place, 238.21 feet along Empire Boulevard, 200
feet on the east, and 221.51 feet on the west. The diagram leaves no residual
part of old lot 28 outside the four tentative lots. The trapezoid area is about
38,119 square feet, within one square foot of the recorded 38,118. The ACRIS
index independently dates the document and records it against lot 28.

MapPLUTO 26v2 still carries only old lot 28, with the same 38,118-square-foot
recorded area. Thus the absence of lots 20, 25, and 30 from that release is a
later-map lag, not evidence for additional land outside old lot 28. No other
live filing was found on this old parcel in the saved 32,015-job filing search.

**Proposed classification:** resolved at **38,118 recorded square feet**. The
economic-parent footprint and the zoning lot coincide with the one pre-existing
tax lot. This does not establish four separate legal wage-assessment units.

## 137 Beach 30, 139 Beach 29, and 147 Beach 30

The saved economic parent has jobs Q01177691 (99 units, filed February 11,
2025), Q01177738 (74, also February 11), and Q01222315 (57, filed May 13). The
filing lots are Queens 15822/48, 15821/50, and 15822/44. The 139 Beach 29 job's
reported lot is old lot 15821/46, so its filing/reporting crosswalk depends on a
later split.

At the 23v3.1 reference date, the three later filing polygons fall within old
lots 15821/46, 15822/44, and 15822/48. Their recorded areas were 20,817, 9,000,
and 23,000 square feet, totaling the 52,817 square feet currently carried in
production. The later site uses only 85.4 percent of old lot 46 and 69.4 percent
of old lot 44, while it uses essentially all of old lot 48. Assigning the three
whole old parcels therefore overstates the mapped later site by about 10.9
percent. The broader connected history also includes old lot 15822/45 and old
15823/43, confirming that this was not a clean union of three unchanged lots.

DOF records three relevant apportionments:

- Transaction 381342, entered August 25, 2025, splits old 15821/46 into lots 46
  and 50. It cites the lot-46 deed recorded March 20, 2025 (CRFN
  2025000077073) and an Adrian Lalsa survey dated May 16, 2025.
- Transaction 280940, entered April 4, 2025, reapportions old 15822 lots 23,
  35, 42, and 44 into a row of smaller lots, including multiple lots used by
  the separate two-family filings at 159–175 Beach 30 and 132–142 Beach 31. It
  cites a March 6 deed (CRFN 2025000062897) and a March 12 survey.
- Transaction 370541, entered August 11, 2025, reapportions 15822 lots 44, 45,
  and 48 together with 15823/43 into new lots 44 and 48. It cites deeds recorded
  March 6 and July 16 (CRFNs 2025000062933 and 2025000187428) and a July 29
  survey.

In MapPLUTO 25v4, the three filing lots have recorded areas of **17,780 square
feet (15821/50), 10,807 (15822/44), and 18,779 (15822/48)**. They sum exactly to
**47,366 square feet**. Their mapped polygons sum to about 47,310 square feet.
The recorded-area sum is the appropriate administrative area if the later lots
are accepted; the polygon sum is only a geometry check.

The filing search identifies fifteen additional live two-family jobs in the
connected land history. One is directly inside the earlier parcel union and
the others are connected through the DOF transaction envelope. They should not
be added to the 230-unit economic parent merely because the later tax-lot work
was performed through related transactions. Their presence demonstrates why
the entire old-land envelope cannot be assigned to the three-building parent.

The actual filed site plan for City Planning project 2026Q0143 was downloaded
from the agency's ZAP API and inspected. The November 9, 2025 plan is titled
“Development on Lot 48 and Lot 44,” identifies buildings at 137 and 155 Beach
30th Street, and states a **63,346.60-square-foot zoning-lot area**. Its block
and lot list is 15822/48, 44, 23, 24, 25, 26, 28, 29, and 34 through 42. The
dashed zoning-lot boundary accordingly includes the two large building lots
and a substantial L-shaped group of narrow parcels used for parking and open
space. The plan does not include 15821/50, the filing lot for 139 Beach 29.

This plan is later than the filings, but timing alone does not disqualify it.
Its substance creates the unresolved issue: the two Beach 30th buildings use a
63,346.60-square-foot zoning lot, larger than their two later tax lots, and
many included narrow parcels also carry the separate live two-family filings
found in the filing search. The plan labels building footprints and parking as
illustrative. It therefore documents shared development land but does not
allocate that land exclusively between the 230-unit parent and the other live
projects. Adding lot 50's 17,780 square feet mechanically would produce
81,126.60 square feet, but that would assign all shared zoning-lot land to this
parent without resolving the competing uses.

**Proposed classification:** unresolved. **47,366 square feet** is a valid
later three-tax-lot benchmark, but it omits common/accessory land shown on the
filed plan. **63,346.60 square feet** is the exact two-building zoning-lot area,
but it excludes the third filing and includes land with other live filings.
The missing evidence is an agency record or defensible allocation rule that
links 139 Beach 29 to the two-building site and divides the shared zoning-lot
land among the large buildings and the separately filed small houses.

## Beach easement pages: operative land allocation

The supervisor retrieved the 32-page recorded instrument
`2025071800009001`, titled *First Amendment to Zoning Lot Development and
Easement Agreement*. I rendered and OCRed every page and visually checked the
key RP-602 drawing. The instrument supplies a substantive answer that the
ACRIS index could not.

Pages 5–7 divide the merged zoning lot into three ownership categories. Beach
31 Buyer LLC's “Owner Land” comprises the fifteen narrow lots 23–26, 28–29,
and 34–42. East Coast Developers Group LLC's “Lot 44 Developer Land” comprises
lots 43–45. Beach 30 Multifamily LLC's “Lot 48 Developer Land” is lot 48.
Pages 6–7 explain that an earlier five-lot zoning lot had been subdivided and
that lots 43–45 and 48 were expected to be merged and reapportioned into two
developer lots matching the attached RP-602.

The development-rights chart on page 27 gives anticipated physical lot areas
of 33,751.00 square feet for the Owner Land, 10,807.00 for Lot 44 Developer
Land, and 18,778.60 for Lot 48 Developer Land, totaling 63,336.60 square feet.
It allocates 57 dwelling units to the Lot 44 developer and 99 to the Lot 48
developer. The RP-602 on page 29 visually confirms two proposed developer
lots: proposed lot 44 is a 108.07-by-100-foot rectangle; proposed lot 48 has
184.42 feet along Beach 30th Street, a 105.20-foot rear segment plus a small
notch, and 100-foot depth. The form says present lots 43, 44, 45, and 48 will
be merged/apportioned into requested lots 44 and 48. These are anticipated
areas in the recorded agreement, not a replacement for a final DOF area until
cross-checked against the completed tax-map action.

The fifteen narrow parcels are not conveyed or made exclusive physical
accessory land for the two apartment buildings by this amendment. Pages 7 and
27 reserve to their owner 35,232 square feet of residential floor-area rights,
30 dwelling units, and lot coverage computed as though the Owner Land were an
independent zoning lot. Thirty units equal the capacity of the fifteen saved
two-family filings. Page 8 instead makes the apartment developers responsible
for the merged zoning lot's affordable-housing requirement, including the
requirement generated by those reserved 35,232 square feet, and pages 7–9
subordinate the Owner Land's zoning interests to the development-rights
arrangement. Thus the instrument transfers and regulates zoning capacity; it
does not assign the 33,751 square feet of Owner Land as the apartment
buildings' exclusive usable land. It affirmatively preserves legal development
capacity on those parcels, although it does not prove that the earlier
two-family DOB jobs will actually be built.

Together with the separate Beach 29th City Planning description, the physical
development-site evidence is therefore approximately 18,778.60 square feet
for lot 48, 10,807.00 for proposed lot 44, and 17,779 square feet for Beach
29th lot 50: 47,364.60 square feet before rounding and final tax-map checks.
This closely explains the saved 47,366-square-foot three-lot benchmark while
excluding the fifteen separately retained small-house parcels. Whether to
adopt that benchmark remains the supervisor's adjudication.

### Beach 29th companion agreement contradiction check

I inspected all 23 pages of recorded zoning-lot development agreement
`2026061000055017`. It does not contradict the physical three-development-
parcel boundary, but it clarifies that lot 50 uses zoning capacity generated
by retained lot 46.

Pages 3–4 again define lot 46 as the Owner Premises and lot 50 as the Developer
Premises. Pages 4–7 transfer to the lot 50 developer all existing development
rights generated by lot 46 and previously allocated to former lot 46, while
the owner retains only bonus development rights that may arise after the
agreement's effective date. That is a zoning-capacity transfer, not a transfer
of fee title or a general right to occupy lot 46.

The only physical cross-parcel rights are narrow construction and maintenance
rights. Page 10 grants permanent support and waterproofing easements on the
Owner Premises and a non-exclusive license for window and facade work. Pages
10–11 also allow temporary access to cure a zoning violation. The agreement
contains no shared parking, driveway, ingress/egress, open-space, yard, or
landscaping easement over lot 46 for the apartment building. Page 12 instead
states expressly that each party retains full ownership and control of its
premises, that the buildings are to be filed as separate and independent, and
that the parcels remain separate tax lots. Page 17 assigns any unidentified
strip or gore between the two premises to the Developer Premises, but the
recorded metes and bounds on pages 21 and 23 meet at the same 329.24-foot point
and reconstruct the full 200-foot former-lot frontage, so the exhibits show no
such gap.

The agreement therefore confirms a distinction central to this review: lot 46
is part of the combined zoning lot and supplies development rights, but it is
not documented as common ground or general usable land for the lot 50
building. Nothing in its operative terms expands the measured lot 50 physical
site beyond the 170.76-by-104.12-foot developer parcel.
