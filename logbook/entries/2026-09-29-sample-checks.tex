\section*{September 29, 2026: Checks of the main model's sample}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry.}

Jacob asked for three checks of the main model's sample. Each re-estimates the
scaled burden by likelihood on its full grid, recalibrates the weights and
bootstraps it 500 times, exactly as for the main sample.
\path{prepare_estimation_sample.R} writes every variant to
\path{estimation_parents.parquet}, and the two bootstrap scripts take the
variant as their argument.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A common horizon} (\texttt{horizon\_180}). Most recent parents
  have been observed for less than a year, so later buildings may still be
  unfiled. This check counts only buildings filed within 180 days of a parent's
  first filing, in both periods, and keeps only recent parents observed for at
  least 180 days.
\item \textbf{2025 parents only} (\texttt{cohort\_2025}). These are the recent
  parents first filed in 2025, which have the longest follow-up.
\item \textbf{A pre-policy placebo} (\texttt{placebo}). Historical parents
  first filed in 2015--2018 stand in for the historical sample, and those
  first filed in 2019--2022, before 485-x, stand in for the recent one. The
  model should find no burden. \path{build_estimation_panels} now also writes
  panels for 2015--2022, which provide these parents; the canonical panels are
  unchanged.
\end{itemize}

Units lost are scaled to each variant's recent parents of at most 300 units,
so the table also reports them per 100 of those parents.

The per-parent loss is a little smaller on the common horizon and among 2025
parents, but it stays well within the main interval. So the main estimate is
not produced by the short follow-up of recent parents. The placebo finds no
burden: 87 percent of its draws choose a zero jump, and its upper bound of 89
units per 100 parents is below the main lower bound of 163. The shift from
2015--2018 to 2019--2022 therefore does not look like a notch at 100. It does
leave more unexplained, though ($\varepsilon = 0.075$ against 0.015). The
placebo's splitting cost and cost growth are not identified, because without a
burden nothing favors splitting.

On the main sample the three bootstrap reports match their earlier versions
apart from their names.

\paragraph{Follow-up: the condominium screen before 2019 and a longer history.}
Jacob asked whether 2011--2022 as the historical period changes the results.
Two problems came up. Residential floor-area ratio, a calibration variable,
first appears in the 13v1 PLUTO release, so parents first filed before 2014
cannot be weighted like the others. And the Attorney General searches that
screen for condominiums had been run for 2019--2022 filings but not for 1,035
filings of 2011--2018 parents. A parent with any unsearched filing was
unresolved and dropped, and parents with several filings were dropped more
often. Among 2014--2018 parents of at least 50 units kept in the sample, 8.4
percent had more than one building, against 13.4 percent of all such parents.

Jacob approved running the missing searches. \path{fetch_nys_ag_offering_plans}
publishes them as the September 29, 2026 capture, made with the new
\texttt{early} scope of its query scripts. In the classification, 844 of the
parents they concern become plausible A/B rentals, 26 homeownership (Option D)
and 11 not exposed. The 99 that stay unresolved are government-owned. No other
parent's classification changed, and neither did the 2019--2022 comparison.
The kept 2014--2018 share with several buildings rises to 9.9 percent. The rest
of the gap comes from more condominium and government projects, which are
correctly outside the rental sample.

The extended panels of \path{build_estimation_panels} (renamed from the placebo
panels) now start in 2014, and \texttt{history\_2014} adds 1,142 historical
parents first filed in 2014--2022 as a fifth check. With the corrected screen,
the placebo baseline has 393 parents instead of 327.

The longer history leaves units lost almost unchanged. Before the new searches
it gave 782 (438--1,535). The placebo still finds no burden, now with a tighter
upper bound. Its unexplained share is unchanged, so the undercount of
multi-building parents did not cause it. The main, 180-day and 2025 results
reproduce exactly.

\paragraph{Splitting by year.} \path{plot_splitting_by_year.R} in
\path{plot_bunching} plots the share of A/B parents of at least 50 units with
more than one building, by year of first filing. It is 10--16 percent in every
year from 2014 to 2022, about 28 percent in 2025 (27 percent counting only
buildings filed within 180 days), and 17 percent in 2026, whose later
buildings may still be unfiled. In the estimation sample the increase over
2019--2022 is 8.5 points (95 percent interval 3.0--14.0, $p = 0.001$), or 8.2
points against the reweighted historical share (bootstrap interval 3.1--13.6).
It is 14.8 points for 2025 parents and 12.9 points on the 180-day horizon.
Jacob will use it with the bunching to motivate the model.
