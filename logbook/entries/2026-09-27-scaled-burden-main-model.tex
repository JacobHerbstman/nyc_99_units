\section*{September 27, 2026: The scaled burden as the main model}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry.}

Jacob made the scaled burden the main model for now: each parent's jump and
kink are multiplied by one lognormal scale with median 1, estimated by
likelihood with the unexplained share $\varepsilon$. It nests the single burden,
fits better than it or non-optimizers, and is where the reserved wage test
would enter. The least-squares single burden is kept as the robustness
specification \texttt{least\_squares} (formerly \texttt{main}); renaming
changed no result.

The largest misses are not responses to the threshold. Small parents with
several buildings, which the policy gives no reason to reorganize, are half as
common after the policy as before (2.2 against 4.2 percent in two buildings),
and the model, which leaves such parents as they were, overpredicts them and
the three-building parents. The difference persists with the common 180-day
horizon (2.4 against 5.1 percent of weighted parents under 100 units with
several buildings), so it is not late companion filings. Its mirror image is
the shortfall of small single buildings (55.1 against 50.1 percent under 99).
The splitting the policy causes, two buildings of 100--197 units, is close
(11.5 against 10.1). The model also puts too few parents at 99 and at 95--98
(3.2 percent observed against 1.0), since it sends every shrinking parent to
exactly 99, and keeps too many large single buildings (10.1 against 7.9).

\paragraph{Open questions.} In order of what they could change:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Whether the linkage is equally able to find small multi-building
parents in both periods. Historical parents also draw on evidence the recent
ones lack (Housing Database and parcel adjacency, reviewed links). If the
difference is linkage, the benchmark's building counts are not comparable.
\item Whether some affected parents are not built, or all project sizes
shifted between periods. Either would explain fewer large single buildings and
more small ones than the model predicts; exit would raise units lost, a common
shift would lower the policy's share of the change.
\item Whether parents that shrink land a few units below 99, which the model
could allow with a small landing error; this changes units lost little.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{September 27 follow-up: the linkage is not the reason}

Jacob asked to check the first open question before changing the model. The
checks were run interactively on the production outputs and are not saved as
an audit.

\paragraph{The evidence is the same in both periods.} The small multi-building
parents are held together by the owner companion rule: 24 historical and 7
recent parents under 100 units, whose 119 internal links nearly all carry
same-owner evidence. Keeping only owner, same-lot, explicit-reference and
reviewed links, and dropping parcel adjacency and lot history (which for recent
filings rely on the older 23v3.1 parcel map), splits one parent in each period
and no small one. The DOB system does not matter either: 2019--20 filings come
from BIS and 2021--22 filings from DOB NOW, as the recent ones do, and the
small multi-building share is flat across the four historical years (5.0, 4.8,
5.5 and 4.1 percent of parents of at most 300 units).

\paragraph{The recent shortfall is concentrated in 2026 parents.} Among
parents first filed in 2025 the share is 3.7 percent (6 of 161), within
sampling noise of the historical level; among 2026 parents it is 0.9 percent (1
of 117). The 2026 parents are also less often multi-building of any size (12.0
against 24.8 percent). Late companions explain part of this: with 2025's
companion timing, about 79 percent of multi-building parents would already
show their last building at the follow-up of a 2026 parent, which predicts
about 20 percent, not 12. Separately, the smallest filings became rarer:
filings of 6--19 units fell from 286 a year in 2019--2022 to 209 a year in the
recent period, while 20--49-unit filings rose from 128 to 176.

\paragraph{Consequences.} The benchmark's building counts are comparable; the
misfit on small multi-building parents is a mix of short follow-up for 2026
parents, fewer tiny filings and noise, and concerns parents the policy does
not affect. The larger concern is splitting itself: if 2026 parents are
incompletely observed or behave differently, pooling them understates the
splitting response. The next checks are the main model on the common 180-day
horizon and on 2025 parents alone. Whether anything changed for 2026 filings,
such as the Comptroller's proposed 485-x rules of early 2026, is unverified.
