\section*{September 14, 2026: Exploratory flat-charge pilot of the joint size and organization model}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry, consolidating follow-ups through
September 22.}
\input{archive/2026-09-22-prepolicy-pilot/pilot_values.tex}

\paragraph{Status.} These are exploratory estimates, not current results.
Every pilot number below comes from the build archived in
\path{logbook/archive/2026-09-22-prepolicy-pilot/}, with 554 historical and
310 post-policy parents. That build predates the September 22 correction that
moved historical proposals to the 23Q4 Housing Database. The corrected
weighting sample has 595 historical and 310 post-policy
parents, and its reweighted historical exact-99 share is 0.52 percent,
against 1.35 percent in the archive. No model has been fit to the corrected
panel; a new fit waits for the land and boundary work. The original September
14 run on 561 historical and 316 post parents is superseded, and its numbers
are not repeated here.

The pilot reproduces the aggregate 99-unit spike but not how parents organize
into filings. It predicts too few two-filing parents, far too many with three
or more, almost none of the observed $99+99$ parents, and almost no movement in
the upper tail. It accounts for about 11 percent of the raw weighted unit gap.

\paragraph{Model and what is fixed.} Jacob requested a first estimation with
the marginal-cost parameter fixed at $\tau=0$, so the policy is a pure notch.
Curvature is fixed at $\lambda=1$: moving a parent from its preferred total $x$
to $m$ units costs $((m-x)/99)^2$ in normalized profit units. Under separate
assessment, each constituent filing with 100 or more units pays a flat charge
$\kappa$. Sizes are positive integers and layouts are unrestricted, so a parent
of $m$ units in $J$ filings pays nothing when $m\le 99J$ and $\kappa$
otherwise. Holding $J$ fixed, a parent shrinks to $99J$ exactly when
$x\le 99J+99\sqrt{\kappa}$; above that it keeps $x$ and pays.

Each weighted historical parent supplies $x$, its filing count $J_0$, and its
constituent vector. Its own $J_0$ carries no organization disadvantage; every
other count from one to six draws an independent exponential disadvantage with
mean $\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}$, the same at every size and layout. Parcel counts
do not restrict the menu. The number of parents, the historical weights, and
each parent's land footprint are held fixed. Only $\kappa$ and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}$ are estimated.

The objective is the unweighted sum of squared share errors over 45 disjoint
cells: one, two, or three-plus filings crossed with 15 size bins (under 50,
50--89, 90--94, 95--98, 99, 100--104, 105--119, 120--149, 150--179, 180--197,
198, 199, 200--249, 250--300, 301+). The fit targets joint masses; it does
not estimate a cutoff from the first observed project above 99. The baseline
searches 572 $(\kappa,\sigma_{\mathrm{org}})$ pairs: 26 values of $\kappa$
from 0 to 1 and 22 of $\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}$ from 0.0025 to 10, dense over
$\kappa\in[0.07,0.13]$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}\in[0.6,1.6]$. Each
sensitivity searches 180 pairs.

\paragraph{Calibration and the given footprint.} Both samples keep
\texttt{included\_ab} rental parents with at least 50 units and composition
eligibility, first filed in 2019--2022 against January 1, 2025 through July 8,
2026. Housing Database Class A units take priority, with DOB fallback only
when that count is missing. Historical weights balance four site
characteristics: log lot area, residential FAR, built FAR, and borough. Land
area sums the distinct matched lots; both FARs are land-weighted averages over
those lots. Historical parcels come from filing-specific lagged maps and post
parcels from the fixed 2023 map.

Multi-lot status is not a calibration moment. Codex first recommended keeping
it, with the four-moment weights as a sensitivity. Jacob raised the concern
that multi-lot status is likely affected by the policy, and the baseline was
changed to the four moments above. Lot counts and filing configurations stay
in the data as observed development choices. Jacob also specified that the
parent's land footprint is taken as given: the developer chooses units and
organization within it, and historical organization, including ordinary
multi-filing parents, supplies the counterfactual. One caveat remains open:
the parcel attributes predate the filing, but which lots enter a parent's
aggregate can reflect a later assembly decision.

In the archived weights, every weight is positive, the effective historical
sample size is 493.8 of 554, and the largest moment error is
$6.5\times10^{-10}$. The weighted historical multi-lot share is 4.1 percent,
against 11.0 percent after the policy.

\paragraph{Headline fit.} The best baseline point is $\kappa=\NotchKappa$ and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}=\NotchSigma$, with objective 0.00633.

Scaled to 310 parents, the weighted historical benchmark proposes 46,928
units, the model 46,433, and the post period 42,341. The model therefore
implies \NotchUnitLoss{} fewer proposed units, against a raw benchmark-minus-post
gap of \NotchDirectGap{}. Allowing reorganization preserves
\NotchReorganizationUnits{} units relative to holding each parent's filing
count fixed. All of these condition on the number of parents. The unexplained
part of the gap can reflect model or counterfactual misspecification; it does
not separately identify compression, entry, or completed housing.

\paragraph{Where it fits poorly.} The first failure is the $99+99$
allocation. Nine of the 310 post parents are exactly $99+99$; the model
predicts 0.19 percent, and the 198 two-filing cell is among the largest misses
in the heat map ($-2.7$ points). The flat charge and independent organization
draws cause this. For a 140-unit single-filing parent, every count from two
to six removes the charge without any size loss, so a reorganizing parent
spreads over all five. A 210-unit parent can avoid the charge at full size
with three filings, so $99+99$ appears only when the two-filing draw happens
to be low. The model matches the total multi-filing share (16.7 percent
fitted, 16.8 observed) but places it wrongly: 12.3 percent with three or more
against 3.55 observed, with about 3 percent each at five and six filings.

The size distribution misses in a pattern: single filings are underpredicted
at 50--89 units ($-3.2$ points) and 95--98 ($-2.2$) and overpredicted at
150--179 ($+2.7$), and at 105--179 units there are too few pairs and too many
three-plus parents.

The upper tail barely moves: 9.84 percent of fitted parents exceed 300 units,
against 9.95 in the benchmark and 7.74 observed, and units above 300 per
parent fall only from 51.7 to 51.3, against 38.1 observed. Four post parents
have two or more constituents of at least 100 units, which the model never
chooses because one constituent can absorb all taxed units; they need a
feasibility and legal-assessment check before counting against the model.

The sensitivities fit worse and predict at most 0.17 percent $99+99$. Joint assessment ($\kappa=0.10$,
objective 0.01097, 571 units) leaves historical organization unchanged and
cannot identify an organization cost. Curvature $\lambda=0.5$ gives
$\kappa=0.025$ (0.00661, 374 units), $\lambda=2$ gives $\kappa=0.30$ (0.00690,
458), and a seven-filing menu gives $\kappa=0.075$ (0.00677, 400). All are
mechanical single-threshold schedules at 100 units; the geographic 150-unit
regime and verified legal assessment units are not modeled.

Identification is weak in finite samples. With exact synthetic moments the
pilot recovers both test pairs ($\kappa=0.02,\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}=0.025$ and
$\kappa=0.10,\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}=0.05$) uniquely, but across ten simulated
samples per pair the first is recovered exactly twice, with $\kappa$ from
0.005 to 1, and the second never, with five draws at $\kappa=1$, the top of
the grid. Uncertainty from the estimated weights is not propagated.

\paragraph{Refit history.} The specification never changed; it was refit as
the panels were corrected. On September 20, after Metropolitan Park's 1,000
hotel rooms and three other zero-Class-A parents left the post sample (316 to
312 parents), the refit gave $\kappa=0.115$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}=1.2$, 546
units lost, and a raw gap of 4,471. That correction moved the upper tail and
calibration, not the exact-99 count. After five parent mergers on September
21 (554 and 310 parents) and further covariate-only land corrections, the
archived September 22 build gives $\kappa=\NotchKappa$,
$\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}=\NotchSigma$, \NotchUnitLoss{} units lost, and a raw
gap of \NotchDirectGap.

\subsection*{Follow-ups through September 22}

\paragraph{Adding the multi-lot moment.} Recalibrating to the post multi-lot
share raises the two-filing share but worsens the fit. The best point becomes
$\kappa=0.115$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{org}}=1.3$; the objective rises 23.8 percent,
from 0.00633 to 0.00783, mostly in single-filing cells (0.00275 to 0.00434).
Fitted pairs rise from 4.43 to 8.07 percent and three-plus falls only from
12.30 to 11.34, against 13.23 and 3.55 observed; $99+99$ stays at 0.15
percent. The implied loss is 485 units, but the raw gap nearly doubles, from
4,587 to 8,603, and effective sample size falls to 445.1. The gain in pairs
comes mainly from the counterfactual: the weighted historical two-filing share
rises from 3.09 to 7.40 percent. Whether the matched parcel set belongs to the
given site or responds to the policy remains open; the adopted calibration is
unchanged.

\paragraph{Where the raw gap sits.} Parents above 300 units account for 4,215
of the 4,587-unit gap, or 92 percent. Each bar below sums units within a size
bin, with the weighted historical distribution scaled to 310 parents; positive
bars mean more units in the benchmark.

The benchmark has 30.8 parent equivalents above 300 units against 24 observed,
with mean sizes of 519.6 and 492.0. Valuing the 6.8-parent difference at the
observed mean gives 3,365 units; the difference in means gives the other 851.
Smaller bins offset each other, including excess post units at 99 ($-3{,}248$)
and 198 ($-1{,}782$). The accounting does not follow projects: a project
shrinking from 320 to 198 units also lowers the tail count. The tail-count
difference has a 95 percent percentile bootstrap interval of $[-3.4,17.6]$
parent equivalents, from 493 of 499 draws that recalibrated successfully; it
ignores link uncertainty and common developer shocks. Codex recommends reporting the tail as a
distribution difference without policy attribution. The historical share
above 300 is 12.2, 14.3, 20.3, and 9.6 percent in 2019--2022; the 2021 peak
precedes the June 15, 2022 421-a commencement deadline, but a filing date is
not a commencement date, so an expiration rush remains a hypothesis.

\paragraph{Parents with three or more filings.} Jacob asked for this
inspection before any information from larger parents was set aside. Ten of
the eleven post parents with at least three filings have every constituent
below 100 units. Five repeat exact 99s: two $99+99+99$ triples (Third Avenue;
Bruckner/Brook/East 132nd), $99+99+99+70$ (Wyckoff/Bergen), $99+99+99+99$
(Sullivan/Empire), and $99+99+99+99+99+96$ (165th Street). None of the eight
historical three-plus parents contains an exact 99. All five
repeated-99 parents exceed 249 units, so a fit restricted to 50--249 would
drop all of them; extending to 300 would still drop the 367-, 396-, and
591-unit cases. Codex recommends keeping this organization information in the
fit. The vectors do not identify counterfactual sizes. A 297-unit triple could
have shrunk from above 300 cheaply: moving from 320 to 297 costs
$((320-297)/99)^2\simeq0.054$, below the fitted charge, while moving to 198
costs 1.52. A below-100 vector does not establish legal exemption; of the
five, only Wyckoff/Bergen is classified as verified separate 485-x units.

\paragraph{A local unit quantity.} Jacob requested a unit quantity centered on
counterfactual sizes up to 250, with larger parents kept in the organization
fit. Grouping $310\sum_i\sum_J w_i p_{iJ}(x_i-m_{iJ})$ by counterfactual $x_i$,
parents at 50--250 (266.4 parent equivalents, 35.5 of which shrink) contribute
488 units, 251--300 contribute 0.2, and 301+ contribute 6.9; only 0.35 parent
equivalents leave the 301+ bin. The 250 cutoff is an exploratory reporting
choice. For comparison, assigning the 32.8 excess parents at 99 uniformly to
origins 100--131 and the 9 at 198 to 199--230 (the fitted shrink ranges)
gives about $541+149=690$ units. That illustration attributes all excess mass
to the policy; it is neither an estimate nor a bound.

\paragraph{Parcel structure of the repeated-99 parents.} The five parents
hold 20 filings and 1,948 units. Four (all but 165th Street) have recorded
instruments describing one zoning lot containing several existing or
tentative tax lots; Bruckner's was recorded July 10, after the sample
endpoint. Against 2023 PLUTO, production land misses 4,000 square feet at
Wyckoff/Bergen (one of three old lots matched) and 2,692 at Third Avenue
(three of five). At 165th Street the eight associated parcels (111,540 square
feet) include an MTA terminal; the archived fit uses the adopted
39,349.5-square-foot residential allocation from six of them. Wyckoff/Bergen
has four filings on three old lots and Sullivan/Empire four on one, so a cap
of one filing per old lot would exclude both observed proposals.
Under \href{https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPT/485-X}{RPTL 485-x},
DOB jobs and tax lots do not by themselves establish separate wage assessment.
Codex recommends treating
the outer footprint as given and internal subdivision and building count as
choices, with corrected land informing feasibility and organization costs.

\paragraph{Proposed next modeling steps.} These are the recorded plan and
Codex recommendations, not adopted specifications; all wait for the corrected
23Q4 panel and the land work. Keeping the adopted sample, weights, and 45
cells, first replace the independent per-count draws with organization costs
that rise with each added filing, still with the flat charge, to address the
excess five- and six-filing parents and the missing pairs. Then replace the
flat charge with one-parameter threshold burdens that grow linearly or
quadratically with constituent size above 99. Larger parents stay in the
organization fit, with the local 50--250 quantity reported separately.
Uncertainty from the weights, the 150-unit geographic regime, and verified
legal assessment units remain to be added.
