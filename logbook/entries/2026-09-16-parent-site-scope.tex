\section*{2026-09-16: Full-sample site checks leave 160 unresolved parents}
\textit{Agent-drafted research entry.}

The current-data pass leaves 160 of the 877 weighting-eligible parents
unresolved, or 18.2\%. The other 717 pass the stated parcel and filing checks.
Of the original 166 DOF-flagged parents, 19 now pass and 147 remain unresolved.
Checking the broader sample finds 13 additional cases. The denominator is the
canonical rental-opportunity sample with at least 50 units and complete
production weighting covariates; eighteen other rental parents of this size

The unresolved parents contain 28,443 historical units and 9,806 post-policy
units, together 27.2\% of proposed units in the checked sample. Eleven
post-policy unresolved parents contain an exact-99 constituent. The remaining
land questions are therefore quantitatively relevant to estimation.

A mutually exclusive primary reason assigns 67 parents to partial earlier
parcels requiring allocation, 55 to shared earlier land or other filings on
the site, 16 to incomplete later parcel maps, ten to source or reference-date
disagreements, six to other filings connected only through a broader DOF
transaction, five to incomplete earlier geometry or area, and one to a
conflicting DOB original-lot list. The underlying table preserves overlapping
flags. These are review questions, not 160 confirmed data errors or a conclusion
that each requires separate manual research.

The calculation extends the geometry comparison to all 877 parents and searches
32,015 saved New Building jobs from the Housing Database and DOB NOW. The
search includes small projects, observed nonresidential filings, and earlier
filings with construction recorded beyond the reference date. Known withdrawn,
cancelled, and superseded applications do not count as additional live projects.
Root job numbers link the broad sources to canonical cohort parents, preventing
a filing's historical and recent linkage-universe representations from creating
false companions. Exact parcel links, filing points, and transaction-only
connections remain separate evidence. A fully mapped outside parent can clear
a weak point or transaction connection when its whole footprint is separate.

There are 1,123 parent--filing matches, including own filings and older context.
Across 63 sites, 96 distinct other live filings warrant review; twelve report
zero dwelling units and two descriptions mention a school. At Flatbush, the
saved sources identify the later 521-unit filing through the connected lots.
At Suffolk and Norfolk, the shared parcel and zoning lists keep the allocation
question explicit. The search does not merge parents on adjacency or a common
transaction.

Two dated checks reduce map gaps without individual exceptions. Four parents
using tabular PLUTO 18v1 recover geometry from MapPLUTO 18v1\_1: all requested
lots are present, the recorded area totals agree, the map predates filing,
and DOF records no intervening physical change. Original reference areas remain
unchanged. For the Boyland pair and Beach 28 Street pair, the parent contains
all successor lots in a completed DOF subdivision. Reversing the complete
transaction recovers exactly the mapped earlier lot set, preserving the
20,000- and 52,380-square-foot areas despite missing later MapPLUTO polygons.
Partial successor sets cannot pass that rule.

\subsection*{Candidate corrections and the interpretation of a passing site}

A parent passes when its complete filing site corresponds to whole earlier
parcels, with no unresolved source or filing-scope conflict. Map alignment
allows at most 2\% of each union outside the other; complete DOF successor
sets provide the separate route described above. Tightening map tolerance to
1\% or 0.5\% raises the unresolved count to 165, with the transaction route
unchanged. The candidate area sums
recorded reference tax-lot areas, counting each parcel once. This preserves
the administrative area convention. Shoreline-clipped geometry is a boundary
check and remains distinct from a surveyed dry-land or buildable-area measure.
For example, 18 India Street's recorded area and clipped polygon have different
shoreline scope. Ownership and acquisition dates, and legal wage-assessment
units, require separate evidence.

Among the 717 passing parents, 236 have a candidate recorded area differing
from production by more than one square foot: 202 historical and 34
post-policy parents. The remaining 481 agree within one square foot.
Examples are 567 Fulton Street, from 279 to 27,542 square feet; 2508 Foster
Avenue, from 1,360 to 11,680; and 643 Degraw Street, from 1,043 to 9,169.
Recovering full earlier parcel sets can materially change land covariates even
when the allocation question is resolved under this rule. These candidate
corrections remain in the audit; production areas and weights have not been
revised or refitted.

The Housing Database is not a complete inventory of legacy nonresidential
filings. DOB NOW contributes such jobs from its saved coverage, and only
fourteen parents have the captured detailed BIS site fields. A filing point
locates a record but does not draw a building's boundary. A passing screen
therefore means support from the available records and the specified land
convention; it is not independent certification of complete legal coverage or
original ownership. The unresolved categories identify the questions for
subsequent documentary research. No additional web research was conducted in
this pass.
